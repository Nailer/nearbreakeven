# NEAR is becoming a cross-chain toll business. Its toll already pays for 15.7% of new NEAR.

> Generated by `pnpm thesis` from the run with data through **2026-11-12** (UTC). Verdict: **HOLDS**.
> Snapshot SHA-256 `784a00b915b0fd20b1c06abc6f826408f68e367b1d8dec7f23d247548c053be3` · results SHA-256 `7ef91d4c95a119a3a5c081e768d14fc66ef97029d356c76d5761043b6357f179`.
> Anchored on NEAR testnet at `ledger.nearbreakeven.testnet`. Every number below comes from that run; none is typed by hand. Not investment advice.

## In brief

Since the 2024-07 peak, NEAR's daily active accounts fell 96.8% and transactions 91.9%. Over the same stretch NEAR Intents, the layer that settles swaps between chains, grew into NEAR's main source of revenue: $1.84M in the last 30 days, 13.97× all the gas paid on NEAR L1, and NEAR uses it to buy NEAR back. The right scoreboard for NEAR is no longer active accounts. It is coverage: how much of the value of newly issued NEAR that revenue pays for. Coverage is 15.7% over 30 days and rose from 11.2% to 18.1% quarter on quarter.

## The claim

Long-term, NEAR's value depends on Intents revenue catching up with issuance, not on L1 activity. At today's take rate (4.87 bps) and price, Intents needs about $803.57M of volume a day to pay for all new NEAR: 6.39× today's $125.81M. If last quarter's growth in coverage repeated, that happens in 4 quarters.

## The four tests

| | Claim | Passes if | Measured | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | NEAR's revenue engine has moved from L1 gas to NEAR Intents. | Intents revenue over the last 30 days is at least 5× the gas fees paid on NEAR L1 over the same days. | 13.97× ($1.84M vs $131.5K) | passes |
| C2 | Intents revenue is covering a growing share of NEAR's new issuance. | Coverage (Intents revenue ÷ USD value of newly issued NEAR) over the last 90 days is higher than over the 90 days before. | 18.1% vs 11.2% | passes |
| C3 | At the current pace, Intents revenue pays for all new issuance within two years. | If the last quarter's coverage growth repeats each quarter, coverage reaches 100% within 8 quarters. | 4 quarters | passes |
| C4 | NEAR's price no longer follows L1 activity, so daily active accounts are the wrong scoreboard. | Correlation between month-on-month changes in active accounts and in price, over the last 12 complete months, is below 0.3. | r = -0.307 | passes |

## Evidence for

**The revenue moved (C1).** Intents earned NEAR $1.84M in the last 30 days. Gas on the whole L1 earned $131.5K (29,469.98 NEAR). That is 13.97× (test: at least 5×). All-time Intents volume is $37.61B; the last 30 days were $3.77B, up 47.9% on the same 30 days a year earlier.

**Coverage is rising, even as the price rose (C2).** Coverage over the last 90 days is 18.1%, against 11.2% in the 90 days before. A higher NEAR price makes new issuance more expensive in dollars, which pushes coverage down; the average price over the last 30 days was $4.40, and coverage rose anyway.

**Breakeven is within reach, on paper (C3).** Coverage grew 1.62× in a quarter. Repeated, 18.1% reaches 100% in 4 quarters (test: within 8). The volume route says the same thing differently: 6.39× today's daily volume.

**Price stopped following L1 activity (C4).** Over the last 12 complete months, month-on-month changes in active accounts and in NEAR's price have a correlation of -0.307 (test: below 0.3). NEAR traded at $0.96 on 2026-02-12 and is 278.9% above that low, while active accounts kept falling.

**Issuance was halved, and the chain itself shows it.** Read straight from NEAR mainnet's protocol parameters: maximum inflation was 5% at block 165,000,000 and is 2.5% now. New supply over the last 30 days ran at 2.47% a year. A smaller issuance bill is half of why breakeven is reachable at all.

**Each active account is worth more.** Gas paid per active account per day rose from $0.012 at the 2024 peak to $0.069 now. That fits a network that lost mass-airdrop and bot traffic, not paying users.

## Evidence against

**One quarter is a thin base (C3).** Monthly coverage in 2026 swung between 7.4% and 26.1%. C3 extrapolates a single quarter's growth; a quiet quarter breaks it, and the test is built to show that the moment it happens.

**Buybacks are not burns.** NEAR that Intents revenue buys back stays in the total supply: the buyback wallet holds 2,273,913 NEAR and total supply still grows about 2.47% a year. Coverage measures value returned to holders, not supply shrinking.

**Price sits in the denominator (C2).** Coverage divides revenue by the dollar value of new NEAR. A rally makes coverage look worse and a crash makes it look better, so coverage partly tracks price, not only Intents.

**Revenue arrives in lumps.** DefiLlama counts revenue when it is swept into NEAR's revenue wallets, so single months can mislead (2025-06: $184.12M of volume but $0.00 counted as revenue). The 90-day windows exist to smooth this.

**Users may really be gone (C4).** The 96.8% fall in active accounts could be real users leaving, not only farming ending. Public daily totals cannot tell a bot from a person, and a correlation over 11 monthly changes (r = -0.307) is weak evidence either way.

**The take rate can be competed away.** Revenue is 4.87 bps of volume. If competing cross-chain venues push fees down, volume must grow faster than the 6.39× above.

## What would change my mind

- Intents revenue falls below 5× L1 gas over a 30-day window (C1 fails).
- Coverage over 90 days drops below the 90 days before (C2 fails).
- Coverage stops growing fast enough to reach 100% within 8 quarters (C3 fails).
- Price starts tracking L1 activity again: correlation at or above 0.3 (C4 fails).
- Two failures in one run turn the verdict to BROKEN. The ledger on NEAR keeps every run, so a broken thesis cannot be quietly deleted.

## What this data cannot measure

- Who trades through Intents (how concentrated volume is by route, solver or front-end). That needs the Intents Explorer API, which requires a partner key, so it is out of a keyless re-run.
- Bots versus people in active accounts. That needs per-account analysis (for example the NEAR BigQuery dataset, which bills the person who runs it).
- What NEAR does with bought-back NEAR after it reaches the buyback wallet (held, staked or distributed). We report the wallet's balance, not its intent.

## Method

- Sources: NearBlocks daily chain stats, DefiLlama's NEAR Intents volume, fees and revenue, and NEAR mainnet itself through a public archival RPC. All free, none needs a key.
- Windows: the last 30 days through 2026-11-12 (UTC), the last 90 days and the 90 before. The L1 peak is the highest 30-day average since January 2024.
- Coverage = Intents revenue ÷ Σ(new NEAR each day × that day's price). New NEAR each day is the change in total supply, which already nets out burned gas.
- The four tests and their thresholds were fixed on 13 Nov 2026 after a first look at the data, before the first run was anchored on NEAR, and they do not change between runs.
- Every run pins a NEAR block. Re-reading the chain at that block and the sources up to the same day gives the same snapshot hash; computing the metrics from it gives the same results hash.

Re-run it: `pnpm install && pnpm thesis`, or check the anchored run with `pnpm verify`. See README.md.
